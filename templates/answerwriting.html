15 Marks • 150 Words
The Paleolithic Age marked the earliest stage of human technological and cultural evolution, distinguished primarily by the use of crude chipped stone tools. Explain the major features of this phase.
Model Answer
Introduction

The Paleolithic Age or Old Stone Age represents the earliest phase of human cultural evolution characterized by primitive chipped stone tools, hunting-gathering economy and complete dependence on nature for survival.

Body
  • Humans used crude stone tools such as hand axes, choppers and cleavers.
  • The economy was based on hunting, fishing and food gathering.
  • People followed a nomadic lifestyle and lived in caves and rock shelters.
  • The use of fire marked an important development in human survival.
  • Bhimbetka rock shelters provide important evidence of Paleolithic culture.
Conclusion

Thus, the Paleolithic Age laid the foundation for later human technological and cultural advancement.

20 Marks • 250 Words
The Paleolithic Age in India is broadly classified into Lower, Middle and Upper Paleolithic phases based on variations in stone tool technology and changing climatic conditions. Discuss the characteristics of these phases.
Model Answer
Introduction

The Paleolithic Age in India is divided into Lower, Middle and Upper phases based on technological progress in stone tools and changing climatic conditions during the Pleistocene epoch.

Body
  • Lower Paleolithic phase was characterized by Acheulian hand axes and cleavers.
  • Middle Paleolithic phase witnessed flake-based tools and improved adaptability.
  • Upper Paleolithic phase showed development of blade tools and artistic expression.
  • Bhimbetka caves reflect cultural and cognitive advancement.
  • Climatic changes significantly influenced human adaptation and technological evolution.
Conclusion

Thus, the three Paleolithic phases collectively illustrate the gradual technological and cultural evolution of early human society in India.
